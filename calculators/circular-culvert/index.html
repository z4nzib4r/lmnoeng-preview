Method

How it's calculated

DischargeQ = V A
Manning equationV = (k / n) R2/3 S1/2
Hydraulic radiusR = A / P
Flow areaA = (d2 / 8) (θ − sin θ)
Wetted perimeterP = θ d / 2
Water depthy = (d / 2) [1 − cos(θ / 2)]
Top widthT = 2 √( y (d − y) )
Froude numberF = V √( T / ( g A cos(tan−1 S) ) )

θ is the fullness angle: 0 when empty, π when half full, and 2π when full. Inputs are converted to SI first, so k = 1.0; the US-customary equivalent is k = 1.49. Gravity is g = 9.8066 m/s². Depth, velocity, and discharge searches are solved numerically; the results are accurate to at least eight significant digits, and Copy results keeps all eight.

Limits

Assumptions

  • Uniform, steady flow. The water surface has the same slope as the culvert. That strictly applies to long culverts with a constant cross-section.
  • No inlet effects. For inlet or outlet control, use the culvert design calculator.
  • Partially full. The relative depth y/d must be less than 1.
  • Two possible depths. Between the full-pipe value and the peak, one discharge or velocity occurs at two depths. The calculator shows both.
